### Summary
The speaker argues that AI transformation requires explicit working norms, hands-on leaders, capable builders, and narrowly scoped workflows with measurable baselines. His central distinction is between broad workforce augmentation, whose returns are difficult to isolate, and shared automations whose costs and benefits can be assessed more directly.

### Key Insights
- An AI “manifesto” should define operating expectations beyond governance: how employees use AI, manage inference budgets, and share successes and failures. The speaker says Section revises its principles every six to seven months.
- The speaker treats workforce augmentation as groundwork for larger automation gains. He argues productivity benefits depend on proficiency, tools, culture, and change management; saved time does not automatically become additional output.
- Personal, team, and organizational agents need different ownership, budgets, and oversight. In his framework, individuals manage personal agents; widely relied-upon workflows require increasing product management and hardening.
- He favors one primary everyday LLM per team to support shared practices, but reports substantial disagreement among executives. Keeping agent instructions separate from platforms was discussed as a way to ease migration.

### Actions
- [ ] Draft a one-page AI operating manifesto with your team, specifying usage expectations, budget responsibility, and learning practices to make adoption expectations concrete.
- [ ] Select one narrow, high-impact workflow and measure its current effort and quality; pilot human–agent collaboration to validate benefits before expanding.
- [ ] Inventory running agents, their costs, and whether anyone uses their outputs; validate value before retaining, repairing, or disabling them to reduce wasted spending.

Treat productivity gains as hypotheses.
